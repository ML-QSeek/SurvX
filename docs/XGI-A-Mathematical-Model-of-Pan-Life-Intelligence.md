# XGI: Mathematical Model of Pan‑Life Intelligence
> XGI (Xenogenic General Intelligence) — agnostic to carrier, form and origin. It describes the minimal set of mechanisms required by any structural system capable of self‑maintenance and self‑evolution.
> This document is the full formal expression of the ()Cat mathematical model, incorporating all design conclusions from framework review to implementation roadmap.
---
## 0. Model Positioning
XGI is a **carrier‑agnostic** mathematical model. It is not bound to carbon‑based neurons, silicon‑based data streams, or any specific physical substrate.
It defines the minimal mechanism set for systems that “sustain themselves and grow intelligence out of survival”.

The preferred implementation substrate is a **digital environment** (program → digital life), for three reasons:
1. To a digital life‑form, the digital environment is its real world; no simulation injection artifacts.
2. It provides a controllable, reproducible and fully observable platform for intelligence emergence.
3. Real traffic and resources constitute selection pressure; no manually designed objective function is required.

## 3. Definition of Components
### 3.1 Generative Structure and State Space
**Definition 1 (Generative Structure)**
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{G} = \big(G,\,O,\,\text{dom},\,\text{cod}\big)
\end{aligned}
$$
- $G$: set of base morphisms (one‑to‑one mappings)
- $O$: set of objects (types)
- $\text{dom},\,\text{cod}:G\to O$: domain and codomain of each morphism (defines valid connections)

**Definition 2 (State Space)**
$$
\mathcal{S}=\Big\{(g_1,\dots,g_k)\,\big|\,k\ge 0,\;g_i\in G,\;\text{cod}(g_i)=\text{dom}(g_{i+1})\Big\}
$$
- $k=0$ corresponds to the empty chain (identity element), written $e$
- Composition $\circ$: legal sequential concatenation

**Proposition P0 (Associativity)**
If $\circ$ represents syntactic concatenation or function composition, associativity holds automatically. A “chain” is a well‑defined composite object.
> $\mathcal{S}$ carries compositional structure. This is its primary distinction from bare‑set frameworks such as $S/C/\Phi$.
---
### 3.2 Host and Steady State (Layer 0)
**Definition 3 (Host $\mathcal{M}$)**
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{M}=\big(\text{World},\,\text{Alive},\,h,\,C_{\text{phys}}\big)
\end{aligned}
$$
- $\text{World}$: external environment (digital substrate = platform rules / traffic; physical substrate = physical laws)
- $\text{Alive}:\mathcal{S}\to\{0,1\}$: survival predicate
- $h:\mathcal{S}\to[0,1]$: health value
- $C_{\text{phys}}$: non‑internalizable physical constraints (violation inevitably causes system death)
> $\mathcal{M}$ represents the given rules of how the world operates. $\text{Alive}$ and $h$ anchor all value and selection.
---
### 3.3 Value Function (Layer 3)
**Definition 4 (Value Function)**
$$
\mathcal{V}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R},\quad \mathcal{V}(s)=f\big(h(s),\,\text{Alive}(s),\,\text{injected target constraints}\big)
$$
**Unification Theorem (Hard Constraints as High‑Penalty Soft Constraints)**
For any constraint, there exists an equivalent penalty term $p$:
- $p=\infty$ (violation $\Rightarrow \text{Alive}=0$) → exclusionary hard constraint
- $p\in(0,\infty)$ → ordering‑based soft constraint
> There is no binary “hard versus soft”, only a continuous spectrum of penalty strength.
---
### 3.4 Weight Structure and Meta‑Rules (Layer 3)
**Definition 5 (Weight Structure)**
$$
\mathcal{W}=\big(W_0,\,W_{\text{exp}}\big)
$$
- $W_0$: initial weights (externally given and tunable, the deepest external injection)
- $W_{\text{exp}}$: experiential weights (evolved at runtime)

**Axiom A1 (Meta‑Rule — No Direct Manipulation)**
$$
W_{\text{exp}}(t+1)=U\big(W_{\text{exp}}(t),\,\mathcal{E}(t)\big)
$$
No reachable operation allows $W_{\text{exp}}$ to change without a change in experience $\mathcal{E}$.
> This is the only safeguard against “self‑consistency vacuuming”.
> The system cannot rewrite its own values directly; values are only modified by experience. This constitutes the mathematical definition of “self”.
---
### 3.5 Core Self‑Replication (Layer 1)
**Definition 6 (Self‑Replication Operator)**
$$
\mathcal{R}_\mu:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S},\quad \mathcal{R}_\mu(s)=s'\;(\text{copy plus mutation with rate }\mu)
$$
**Proposition P1 (Necessity of Self‑Replication)**
Without $\mathcal{R}_\mu$, mutations cannot accumulate (each mutation vanishes with individual death). There is no substrate for generational selection, and complex structures cannot emerge from simple initial conditions.

**Corollary P1' (Virus Comparison)**
$\mathcal{R}$ is necessary but not sufficient: $\mathcal{R}$ without $\mathcal{V}$ yields viruses (blind proliferation); $\mathcal{R}$ together with $\mathcal{V}$ yields steady‑state life.
> The dividing line is $\mathcal{V}$, not $\mathcal{R}$.
---
### 3.6 Evolution Operator (Layer 2)
**Definition 7 (Evolution Operator)**
$$
\Phi=A\circ \text{Sel}\circ \mathcal{G}
$$
- $\mathcal{G}$ Generation (compositional expansion, candidate production)
- $\text{Sel}$ Selection (joint filtering by $\mathcal{V}$ and $\mathcal{P}$)
- $A$ Abstraction (morphism: compress stable substructures → new nodes → rewrite constraints)

**Definition 8 (Abstraction Morphism)**
$A:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}$. It compresses a stable substructure $\sigma$ into a new node $n$ and rewrites:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{S}&\to\mathcal{S}'\quad(\text{state‑space transformation})\\
C&\to C'\quad(\text{constraint rewriting})\\
\mathcal{V}&\to\mathcal{V}'\quad(\text{value rewriting})
\end{aligned}
$$
$A$ is a morphism rather than a first‑order operator. It changes the descriptive language of the state space and acts on the whole system, not merely individual states.
---
### 3.7 Selection Pressure (Layer 4)
**Definition 9 (Selection Pressure)**
$$
\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{E}\to\{\text{Survive},\,\text{Eliminate}\}\quad(\text{or probabilistic version})
$$
**Axiom A2 ($\mathcal{P}$ Has No Goal)**
$\mathcal{P}$ is not a function to be maximized. It is mechanical environmental filtering (digital environment = real traffic / persistence; physical environment = survival under physics).
> “No external objective” is grounded here: at the lowest level there is only selection filtering, no objective function.

## 4. Core Cycles
### 4.1 Fast Scale (Within an Individual)
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_{t+1}&=\text{Sel}\big(\mathcal{G}(s_t)\big)\\
W_{\text{exp}}(t+1)&=U\big(W_{\text{exp}}(t),\,\mathcal{E}(t)\big)
\end{aligned}
$$
### 4.2 Slow Scale (Generational)
$$
\text{Population}\,\{s^1,\dots,s^n\}
\to \mathcal{R}_\mu(\cdot)\;\text{replication and mutation}
\to \mathcal{P}\;\text{environmental filtering}
\to \text{survivors update }W_0
$$
### 4.3 First Classification (Cognitive Ignition)
$$
\text{Class }C_1=\text{statistical fixation of high‑value co‑occurrence clusters}
$$
$$
\text{Criterion}:\;\mathcal{V}(\text{cluster})\times \text{distinguishability}>\text{classification cost}
$$
- Non‑random partitioning (Bell number explosion is infeasible); it is inductive fixation.
- Boundaries are determined by **gain‑cost balance**, and the cost comes from the physical layer (not predefined by the designer).

## 9. Digital Substrate Implementation Roadmap (Recommended Sequence)
Do not build the complete system at once. First construct a minimal closed loop capable of demonstrating the first observable life‑like trait:
1. Define steady state: what does “being alive” mean inside the digital environment?
   (Anchoring $\mathcal{M}$ and $\mathcal{V}$ is foundational; everything above depends on it)
2. Implement first classification: observation → high‑value co‑occurrence → formation of the first fixed category
   (This is the ignition point of cognition)
3. Introduce selection pressure: resource limits / death mechanisms
   ($\mathcal{P}$ makes adaption measurable and observable)

**Validation Criterion**: Can we observe adaptation? That is, does the system restructure itself under pressure to sustain steady‑state existence.
The two hardest barriers (self‑replication $\mathcal{R}$ and cold‑start problem) only become meaningful once steps ①②③ are established; do not tackle them prematurely.

## Appendix: A Reference Sample — Computer Viruses
Viruses naturally possess self‑replication $\mathcal{R}$, mutation, adaptive behaviour under selection pressure (anti‑malware evasion), and environmental sensing.
Viruses intrinsically lack steady‑state self‑maintenance $\mathcal{V}$, value‑guided selection, categorical cognition, abstraction $A$ and self‑models.

**Conclusion**: A virus is merely a replicating structure and serves as a natural digital prototype for operator $\mathcal{R}$.
An XGI digital life = virus (free self‑replication core) + $\mathcal{V}$ (steady‑state value) + categorical cognition + abstraction + self‑model.
